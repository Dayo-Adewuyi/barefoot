1.3 NEWBORN EMERGENCIES


SECTION ONE
    An ill newborn can quickly become an emergency, so all newborns should be treated promptly. Immediate
    actions must be taken whenever these danger signs are observed in a newborn:
    1.Abnormal temperature (Cold or Hot).
    2.Poor sucking or inability to suckle.
    3.Breathing problem – fast breathing and severe chest in-drawing.
    4.Yellowness of the hands and body.
    5.Poor feeding or not feeding at all.
    6.Bleeding from the cord or redness around the cord stump.
    7.No movement at all or only on stimulation and convulsion.
    8.There should be no hesitation in initiating appropriate actions or immediately referring newborns, where
    necessary, to the next higher-level facility.

    1.3.1. DIFFICULT BREATHING
    Difficult breathing means the baby has to make some effort to breathe. Rapid breathing for a newborn is
    respiration rate between 40-60 per minute. Difficult breathing in children is an emergency. It may be caused
    by infection, cold. In babies, chest in-drawing, flaring of nose indicate difficult breathing and a danger sign to
    look out for.
