```text
7.0 GERIATRIC HEALTH

    The World Health Organization (WHO) describes the elderly as persons aged 60 years and above. Thus,
    with an increasing number of elderly persons (65 years and above) in the population, the PHC worker will
    be confronted with a growing number of elderly patients. This group of patients is vulnerable and at risk of
    health, nutritional, and social problems. They have spent most of their lives contributing to nation-building,
    either in the public or private sector when they were younger, so they deserve the best care from the
    country they served. There are differences in the signs and symptoms of diseases presented in the elderly
    compared to younger people. Therefore, diagnosis in the elderly may require a more precise history and
    laboratory tests. Due to changes associated with the ageing process, such as difficulty in hearing, speech
    problems, memory loss, and so on, it may be necessary for a family member to be involved in their care. An
    understanding of the needs of the elderly is essential for PHC workers.


    7.1. MUSCULO - SKELETAL SYSTEM DISORDERS
    The musculoskeletal system is the collective term for the interconnected system of muscles, bones, joints,
    ligaments, tendons, and other connective tissues in the body. It provides structural support, allows movement,
    protects internal organs, and plays a vital role in maintaining postures. The muscles, controlled by the nervous
    system to enable various bodily functions, such as walking, lifting, and other forms of physical activity. This
    system is essential for overall body function and mobility.
    As adults age, they begin to lose minerals and living materials from their bones. The bones become weaker
    and even more brittle. This physiological process makes the elderly suffer from more broken bones.
    Osteoarthritis is a disease that occurs with ageing and in this condition normal joint movement becomes
    painful and difficult.
```

```text
Health Education
    1.Explain common causes of muscular pain in aging individuals, such as arthritis, muscle strains, and changes
    in joint flexibility.
    2.Emphasize the importance of maintaining good posture and incorporating gentle exercises to improve
    muscle strength and flexibility.
    3.Educate on the appropriate use of over-the-counter pain relievers and potential side effects. Stress the
    importance of consulting healthcare providers before starting any new medications.
    4.Encourage warm-up exercises before physical activities and proper cool-down routines to prevent muscle
    strains.
    5.Highlight the role of hydration and a balanced diet in supporting overall muscle health.
    6.Discuss the impact of excess weight on joints and muscles, emphasizing the benefits of maintaining a
    healthy weight.
    7.Regular, low-impact exercises like walking, swimming, or taichi to enhance muscle strength and joint
    flexibility.
    8.Teach the importance of pacing activities to avoid overexertion, especially for those with chronic conditions.
    9.Provide tips on maintaining proper ergonomics at home and work to prevent muscle strain.
    10.Emphasize the role of adequate rest and quality sleep in promoting muscle recovery.
    11.Encourage seniors to consult healthcare professionals for personalized advice and treatment plans tailored
    to their specific needs.
    12.Discuss non-pharmacological pain management techniques such as heat or cold therapy, massage, and
    relaxation exercises
```

```text
7.2. CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM DISORDERS
Cardiovascular diseases are conditions that affect the heart and blood vessels. When the heart and blood
vessels are healthy, blood flows smoothly throughout the body. However, sometimes the circulatory system
fails to work properly, and blood may not flow correctly through the arteries and veins, leading to serious
health problems.
Some people are born with cardiovascular problems, but most cardiovascular diseases take years to develop.
This is why there is an increased number of cardiovascular diseases in the elderly. Conditions such as high
blood pressure, arteriosclerosis, atherosclerosis, heart attack, heart failure, and stroke are very common in
this age group. Death from cardiovascular diseases can often be prevented if the disease is detected early
and referred immediately.
 CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM DISORDERS is a general term used to describe a disease of the heart and
blood vessels. It occurs when there is a blockage or interruption of the heart muscles that supplies blood to
the heart due to a buildup of fatty substances inside the artery that leads to hardening or narrowing of the
artery known as atherosclerosis Heart disease is a major cause of disability limiting the activity and eroding
the quality of life of millions of older people. People 65 years and above are much more likely to suffer a heart
attack, stroke coronary heart disease and heart failure due to high blood pressure.
```
