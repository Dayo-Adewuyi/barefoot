6.0 MATERNAL HEALTH

6.1 PRENATAL (ANTENATAL) CARE
Prenatal period is the time between conception and childbirth (pregnancy). It lasts for about 280 days or 40
weeks. It is associated with physiological changes in the woman’s body which enable foetal survival as well
as preparation for labour. The physiological changes are not confirmed to the reproductive organs alone, but
every tissue and organ reacts to the stimulus of pregnancy. The metabolic, chemical, and endocrine balance
of the body is also altered. Many of these changes are regarded as signs and symptoms of pregnancy and can
sometimes manifest as minor discomforts. However, when these conditions become persistent and severe,
they become major disorders that will require prompt medical attention.
Prenatal or antenatal care (ANC) is the advice, supervision and intervention given to pregnant women in
order to obtain and maintain a state of good health throughout the prenatal period. Prenatal care provides a
platform for the health worker to discover any medical, obstetric, and mental problem the woman may have
and determine if she can be able to deliver her baby safely. ANC provides opportunity to screen pregnant
mothers for signs and symptoms of mental health conditions such as; anxiety/depression, involvement in
substance use/abuse and signs of domestic violence, for appropriate management and referral.
The 2016 WHO ANC model aims to provide pregnant women with respectful, individualized, person centered
care at every contact and to ensure that each contact delivers effective, integrated clinical practices (tests
and interventions), provides relevant and timely information, and offers psychological and emotional support
by practitioners with good clinical and interpersonal skills working in a well- functioning health system. The
WHO recommends a minimum of eight contacts; five contacts in the third trimester, two contacts in the
second trimester and one contact in the first trimester. There should be emphasis on quality of care and
great focus should also be maintained on birth planning and emergency preparedness.

6.2 MATERNAL HEALTH (LABOUR AND DELIVERY)



    SECTION SIX
Normal labour is characterized by the onset of strong, painful, and regular uterine contractions and retraction,
resulting in the delivery of a live baby without injury to the mother or baby. Labour progresses in two phases:
latent and active.The latent phase is marked by cervical effacement (0-4cm dilated), while in the active phase,
cervical dilatation occurs much faster. The active phase of labour occurs when the cervix is dilated between
5 and 10cm.
Labour is divided into four stages:

First Stage: This stage begins with the onset of strong, painful, and regular uterine contractions, leading to
the dilation of the cervix and the appearance of the show. It should not last for more than 12 hours.

Second Stage: It starts from full dilatation of the cervix to the expulsion of the baby and should not last
for more than 1 hour.

Third Stage: This stage begins after the delivery of the baby and continues until the complete expulsion
of the placenta and membranes, as well as the control of bleeding, which should not last for more than 30
minutes.

Fourth Stage: It encompasses the first hour after delivery, during which both the mother and baby are
allowed to rest and are closely observed. This period is critical, as complications can occur.
