7.6 MENTAL DISORDERS



SECTION SEVEN
    Mental health problems may not be easily detected in the elderly. Symptoms that may indicate a mental
    health disorder in younger patients can have different causes in older adults. Hopelessness, sadness, and
    crying episodes may indicate depression, while irritability can be an affective symptom of depression. General
    anxiety is the most common mental disorder encountered by the elderly (Stefannacci, 2022).
    The risk of developing mental health disorders increases with age. The WHO estimates that mental health
    problems affect over 20% of the elderly population. Despite this high prevalence, mental health problems are
    under-identified among the elderly.The most common mental health disorders in this age group are dementia
    and depression. Others include anxiety disorders, substance use problems, and self-harm. This group is also
    vulnerable to neglect and maltreatment by family members, which can contribute to the development or
    worsening of mental health challenges. Loneliness and social isolation are key risk factors for mental health
    problems, and one in six elderly individuals experiences abuse, often by their own carers (Stev, 2022).

7.7 ORAL DISORDERS



SECTION SEVEN
    Oral health is a state of being free from chronic mouth and facial pain, oral and throat cancer, oral sores,
    birth defects such as cleft lip and palate, periodontal (gum) disease, tooth decay and tooth loss, and other
    diseases and disorders that affect the oral cavity. The most common complaint is pain, which may or may not
    be accompanied by swelling inside the mouth or on the face. The condition of the mouth also reflects the
    client’s general health, so it should be inspected as part of a physical examination.
    Oral health problems are common in the elderly. About 68% of the elderly have gum diseases and tooth loss,
    and untreated dental issues are found in 96% of the elderly. Early recognition of oral problems with prompt
    attention or referral will reduce morbidity and mortality arising from otherwise simple conditions. Healthy
    teeth and gums make it easy to eat well.

Health Education



    SECTION SEVEN
1. Explain process of ageing.
2. Brush twice daily (morning and last thing before going to bed at night).
3. Encourage regular intake of food rich in calcium.
4. Avoid intake of sugary foods (refined sugar) in-between meals.
5. Encourage to rinse mouth with water after meals.
6. Encourage good personal and oral hygiene.
7. Report any tooth/teeth pains.
8. Advise on regular dental checkup at least twice a year.
9. Discourage use of tooth pick.
