7.2. CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM DISORDERS
Cardiovascular diseases are conditions that affect the heart and blood vessels. When the heart and blood
vessels are healthy, blood flows smoothly throughout the body. However, sometimes the circulatory system
fails to work properly, and blood may not flow correctly through the arteries and veins, leading to serious
health problems.
Some people are born with cardiovascular problems, but most cardiovascular diseases take years to develop.
This is why there is an increased number of cardiovascular diseases in the elderly. Conditions such as high
blood pressure, arteriosclerosis, atherosclerosis, heart attack, heart failure, and stroke are very common in
this age group. Death from cardiovascular diseases can often be prevented if the disease is detected early
and referred immediately.
 CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM DISORDERS is a general term used to describe a disease of the heart and
blood vessels. It occurs when there is a blockage or interruption of the heart muscles that supplies blood to
the heart due to a buildup of fatty substances inside the artery that leads to hardening or narrowing of the
artery known as atherosclerosis Heart disease is a major cause of disability limiting the activity and eroding
the quality of life of millions of older people. People 65 years and above are much more likely to suffer a heart
attack, stroke coronary heart disease and heart failure due to high blood pressure.

Health Education
1.Provide simplified information about the cardiovascular system with emphasis on the importance of heart
health and blood circulation.
2.Explain common risk factors such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, and lifestyle choices
3.Discuss how these factors contribute to cardiovascular issues.
4.Educate on the importance of adhering to prescribed medications, understanding dosage schedules, and
potential side effects.
5.Encourage regular check-ups to monitor medication effectiveness.
6.Emphasize a heart-healthy diet with reduced sodium, saturated fats, and cholesterol.
7.Encourage the consumption of fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean proteins.
8.Provide practical tips for meal planning.
9.Encourage low-impact activities like walking or swimming
10.Stress the importance of regular physical activity for cardiovascular health.
11.Discuss stress-reducing techniques such as mindfulness, deep breathing exercises, or gentle yoga. Chronic
stress can exacerbate cardiovascular issues.
12.Emphasize the detrimental effects of smoking on cardiovascular health.
13.Educate and support for quitting smoking.
14.Provide guidance on achieving and maintaining a healthy weight through a balanced diet and regular
exercise.
15.Teach individuals to monitor vital signs regularly, such as blood pressure and heart rate. Encourage keeping
a health journal to track changes and report them to healthcare providers.
16.Emphasize the importance of a supportive social network.
Encourage family involvement in understanding and promoting cardiovascular health

7.3 GENITO- URINARY DISORDERS
    Genitourinary problems are common in the elderly and can be due to metabolic, ageing changes, infections,
    abnormal growths, etc. Examples include: diabetes, vaginal/uterine prolapsed, enlarged prostate, cancers,
    schistosomiasis etc. Patient may complain of discharge, pain and. burning sensation on passing urine
    (micturition), blood in urine, frequent micturition, and urinary retention (difficulty in passing urine), etc.
    Genitourinary problems refer to conditions that affect the genital and urinary organs or the structures
    associated with them. These problems can involve various parts of the urinary and reproductive systems,
    including the kidneys, bladder, ureters, urethra, prostate (in males), testes, ovaries, and other related structures.
    Some common genitourinary problems include urinary tract infections (UTIs), kidney stones, incontinence,
    erectile dysfunction, prostate issues (such as prostatitis or benign prostatic hyperplasia), sexually transmitted
    infections (STIs), cystitis, urinary retention, and reproductive system disorders (like infertility or menstrual
    disorders in women).
