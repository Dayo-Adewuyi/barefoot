3.0 MIDDLE CHILDHOOD


    SECTION THREE
    3.1 CHILD FIRST VISIT
    This section is for children between the ages of 6 and 12 years. The health worker should be able to carry
    out simple sight and hearing tests to determine if the child has any visual or hearing problems that could
    affect school performance. They must also recognize social problems that might put the child at risk and be
    able to screen for malnutrition. Additionally, they should request genotype and blood group tests so that the
    child and parents are informed, and provide counseling if any abnormalities are detected. The health worker
    should also conduct health and nutrition education, immunize, and protect children from illnesses and other
    conditions relevant to the family. Parents and guardians should be encouraged to take their children for
    regular clinic visits, even if the children are well.

3.2 FEVER



    SECTION THREE
    Fever is the elevation of the body temperature above 37.2 °C. Fever is a sign of infection or loss of body fluid.
    Rapid rise of temperature leads to fits (Convulsion) especially in young children. Some common causes of
    fever in children are malaria, tuberculosis, respiratory, gastrointestinal and other bacterial and viral infections.
    All patients with history of fever should be treated and encouraged to drink plenty of water, other fluids, and
    fruits, and should not be overdressed. High fever should be treated as an emergency.
