1.2. NEWBORN WITHOUT ANY COMPLAINT- THE WELL CHILD



SECTION ONE
    A well-visit, or well-child visit, is a preventative health appointment with the child’s healthcare provider with
    the goal of helping well children to stay healthy in their earliest years, so they can grow into healthy adults.
    The well-child visits concentrate on the whole body, not on sickness, based on the child’s age and unique
    needs. The well-child visit is very important and should always include screening for health problems, growth
    monitoring, and promotion. Health education about nutrition, immunization, and protection from illness and
    other conditions as relevant to the family is given. Mothers should be encouraged to take their children for
    visits while they are well, monthly in the first two years of life and every 3 months thereafter until 5 years old,
    and once a year until the child reaches 21 years. These visits also provide opportunities to monitor mothers
    and talk to them about postpartum depression or anxiety.

Health Education


    SECTION ONE
1. Encourage mothers to:

a. Wash hands with soap and water (or use a hand sanitizer) before handling your baby.
b. Support baby’s head and neck.
c. Never shake baby, whether in play or in frustration.
d. Always fasten baby securely when using a carrier, stroller, or car seat.
e. Avoid rough play with newborns, such as jiggling them on the knee or throwing them in the air.
f. Clean around the cord stump with plain water and blot dry until the stump dries up and falls off.
g. Feed babies whenever they seem hungry. Baby may show you they’re hungry by crying, putting fingers in
their mouth, or making sucking noises. A newborn baby needs to be fed every 2–3 hours.

1.3 NEWBORN EMERGENCIES


SECTION ONE
    An ill newborn can quickly become an emergency, so all newborns should be treated promptly. Immediate
    actions must be taken whenever these danger signs are observed in a newborn:
    1.Abnormal temperature (Cold or Hot).
    2.Poor sucking or inability to suckle.
    3.Breathing problem – fast breathing and severe chest in-drawing.
    4.Yellowness of the hands and body.
    5.Poor feeding or not feeding at all.
    6.Bleeding from the cord or redness around the cord stump.
    7.No movement at all or only on stimulation and convulsion.
    8.There should be no hesitation in initiating appropriate actions or immediately referring newborns, where
    necessary, to the next higher-level facility.

    1.3.1. DIFFICULT BREATHING
    Difficult breathing means the baby has to make some effort to breathe. Rapid breathing for a newborn is
    respiration rate between 40-60 per minute. Difficult breathing in children is an emergency. It may be caused
    by infection, cold. In babies, chest in-drawing, flaring of nose indicate difficult breathing and a danger sign to
    look out for.
