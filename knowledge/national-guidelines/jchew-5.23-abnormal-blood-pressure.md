5.23 ABNORMAL BLOOD PRESSURE



    SECTION FIVE
    Blood pressure refers to the force with which the heart pumps blood into body circulation. Blood pressure
    usually increases with age as the blood vessels become less elastic.Women tend to have lower blood pressure
    than men of the same age. The normal systolic pressure for young adult is 100-120 mmHg. High blood
    pressure is referred to as hypertension. Hypertension is a very common cardiovascular disease affecting a lot
    of people. It is thought to result from the narrowing of the blood vessels. The narrowing causes the heart to
    beat harder in order to pump the blood. Hypertension refers to low blood pressure and can lead to shock
    and organ failure.

    Additional points

    Although the recommendation is for multiple BP measure-
    ments and averaging them (complex computational task) to
    get the final BP value for patients, this may not be practical for
    busy primary care settings. Averaging BP readings is time-con-
    suming and prone to errors in a busy clinic. A practical
    approach may be adopted below:
    If the first blood pressure (BP) is <140/90 mmHg, then no
    other blood pressure measurement is needed during that
    encounter.
    If the first BP is >140/90 mmHg, perform a second BP after
    3-5 mins and use the second reading as the recorded BP for
    the encounter.
    If there is a large difference between the first and second
    readings (>5 mmHg), it is reasonable to do a third measure-
    ment and use the third BP as the recorded BP.

Health Education



    SECTION FIVE
    1.Explain the condition and diagnosis to the patient.
    2.Educate the patient that hypertension is a life-long condition that can be controlled with medications and
    lifestyle changes.
    3.Emphasize that without treatment for hypertension, there is an increased risk of stroke, heart attack,
    kidney failure, vision problems, and death.
    4.Ask the patient for a treatment supporter and elucidate on the importance.
    5.Counsel the patient on stopping tobacco and harmful use of alcohol.
    6.Counsel the patient on stopping tobacco and harmful use of alcohol.
    7.Encourage the patient to increase physical activity to at least 30 minutes daily and get adequate rest.
    8.Counsel the patient to lose weight if overweight, using the BMI and waist circumference as a guide. Aim for
    BMI within 18 – 25kg/m2; or waist circumference <94cm in men, and <80cm in women.
    9.Give dietary counseling (a heart-healthy diet low in salt, trans-fat, and added sugar)
    Eat 5 servings of fruits and vegetables per day.
    Eat nuts, legumes, whole grains, and foods rich in potassium.
    Eat fish at least twice per week.
    Use healthy oils like sunflower, flax seed, soybean, peanut, and olive.
    Limit red meat to once or twice per week.
    Use less salt – restrict to less than 5g daily (1 teaspoon)
    Limit consumption of ultra-processed, canned, and ‘fast’ foods.
    Avoid donuts, cookies, sweets, fizzy drinks, and juice with added sugar.
    10.Counsel on the importance of adherence to medications, clinic, and laboratory appointments.
    11.Educate the patient on self-management practices at home.

5.25 WORM INFESTATION



    SECTION FIVE
    Worm infestation is the presence of large amount of worms in the gut, which causes discomfort to the
    patient. Ascaris (roundworm) is the commonest worm infestation. Heavy roundworm infestation produces
    abdominal pains vomiting, constipation and sometimes obstructions of the intestine. Hookworm infestation
    is common in rural areas where heavy infestation causes blood in stool, anaemia and malnutrition. Severe
    Trichuris (whipworm) infestation causes bloody diarrhea, abdominal pain, anaemia and prolapse of the rectum.
    Threadworm also lives in the intestine and causes itching around the anus. Tapeworms is a flat ribbon-like
    worm that can also be found in the intestine.
