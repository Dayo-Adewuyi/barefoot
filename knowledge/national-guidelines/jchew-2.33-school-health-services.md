SECTION TWO
2.33. HIGH RISK GROUPS (ORPHANS AND VULNERABLE CHILDREN)
An orphan is a child (0-17 years) who has lost one or both parents. A vulnerable child is one who, due to
circumstances of birth or immediate environment, is prone to abuse or deprivation of basic needs, care, and
protection, and thus is disadvantaged relative to his or her peers (FMWA&SD, 2008). A vulnerable child may
lack access to education, health, and other social support, have a chronically ill parent, live in a household
with terminally or chronically ill parent(s) or caregiver(s), live outside of family care (with extended family,
in an institution, or on the street), or be infected with HIV. Additionally, a vulnerable child includes those in
need of alternative family care, those who are sexually abused or neglected, children in hard-to-reach areas,
children with mental disabilities, children affected by armed conflict, and children in need of legal protection.
Most current interventions focus on Orphans and Vulnerable children (OVC) aged 6-17 years, often excluding
children under five years old. In Nigeria, various organizations are involved in OVC work, including international
NGOs (mainly USG and Global Fund implementing partners), local NGOs, faith-based organizations (FBOs),
and community-based organizations (CBOs). Some of the main international NGOs involved in OVC work
include the Columbia University International Center for AIDS Care and Treatment Programs (CU-ICAP),
Catholic Relief Services (CRS), Center for Development and Population Activities (CEDPA), and Winrock
International AIM.

3.0 MIDDLE CHILDHOOD


    SECTION THREE
    3.1 CHILD FIRST VISIT
    This section is for children between the ages of 6 and 12 years. The health worker should be able to carry
    out simple sight and hearing tests to determine if the child has any visual or hearing problems that could
    affect school performance. They must also recognize social problems that might put the child at risk and be
    able to screen for malnutrition. Additionally, they should request genotype and blood group tests so that the
    child and parents are informed, and provide counseling if any abnormalities are detected. The health worker
    should also conduct health and nutrition education, immunize, and protect children from illnesses and other
    conditions relevant to the family. Parents and guardians should be encouraged to take their children for
    regular clinic visits, even if the children are well.
