2.10. MALNUTRITION
    Malnutrition is a state resulting from a deficiency or excess of one or more essential nutrients. Acute
    malnutrition is caused by a decrease in food consumption and/or illness resulting in bilateral pitting
    oedema or sudden weight loss. It is characterized by the presence of bilateral pitting oedema or wasting
    (low Mid Upper Arm Circumference or low Weight For Height). Acute malnutrition comprises of both
    severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). All children under 5 years of age

Correction on the use of RUTF:
1.RUTF is designated for very thin or swollen children only and should not be shared.
2.Sick children may have reduced appetite, so offer small, regular meals of RUTF throughout the day, aiming
for around eight meals if possible.
3.For breastfeeding children, continue breastfeeding and offer breast milk before each RUTF feed.
4.RUTF is the primary food for sick and malnourished children during their time in OTP. Always prioritize
RUTF over other family foods.
5.Ensure children have access to plenty of clean water or breast milk while consuming RUTF, as they may
need more fluids than usual.
6.Wash the child’s hands and face with soap and running water before feeding, and keep food packets clean
and well stored.
7.Caregivers should also wash their hands with soap and running water before feeding the child.
8.Keep sick children covered and warm to prevent them from getting cold quickly.
9.For children with diarrhea, continue feeding them and provide extra food and water to prevent dehydration.
10.Return to the health facility if the child’s condition worsens or if not eating sufficiently.

Health Education
    1.Educate mothers/caregivers on personal, food, and environmental hygiene practices.
    2.Provide guidance on ensuring adequate nutrition for children.
    3.Stress the importance of timely immunizations for children.
    4.Emphasize the need for periodic deworming to prevent parasitic infections.
    5.Encourage the use of Long Lasting Insecticidal Nets (LLINs) to prevent malaria.

    6.Advocate for regular monitoring of child growth and development.
    7.Highlight the significance of maintaining home-based records for tracking the child’s health status and
    upcoming clinic or hospital visits.

2.11. HIV/AIDS
HIV/AIDS is caused by a virus that can be transmitted from infected mothers to their babies during pregnancy,
delivery, or breastfeeding. It’s crucial for HIV-positive mothers to receive guidance on the best feeding
practices to ensure their babies’ survival without HIV transmission. Antiretrovirals can be administered to
reduce the risk of virus transmission. Babies born to HIV-positive mothers are termed HIV-exposed babies,
and a definitive HIV diagnosis can only be made after proper testing and confirmation.
