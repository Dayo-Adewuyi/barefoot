5.3 COUGH
    Cough is a rapid expulsion of air from the lungs in order to clear the lungs and airways of fluids, mucus, food
    particles or other materials. Cough is a sign of irritation of the throat and wind pipe which comes suddenly.
    Causes of cough are: common cold, inhalation of dust, fumes and pollens, heart and lung diseases, cigarette
    smoking, etc. Cough sometimes helps clear the lung of accumulated mucus . It is therefore not good to give
    cough suppressants unnecessarily when a patient has cough.

5.4 DIFFICULTY IN BREATHING
    Difficulty in breathing means the client has to make extra effort to breathe. Rapid breathing in an adult means
    breathing more than 25 times per minute. It is important to observe the client’s breathing carefully as part
    of your general observation. Most common causes of difficult breathing are pneumonia, asthma, tuberculosis,
    bronchitis and heart failure. Wheezing in an adult can be caused by asthma or congestive heart failure.
