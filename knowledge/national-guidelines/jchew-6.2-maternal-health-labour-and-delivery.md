6.2 MATERNAL HEALTH (LABOUR AND DELIVERY)



    SECTION SIX
Normal labour is characterized by the onset of strong, painful, and regular uterine contractions and retraction,
resulting in the delivery of a live baby without injury to the mother or baby. Labour progresses in two phases:
latent and active.The latent phase is marked by cervical effacement (0-4cm dilated), while in the active phase,
cervical dilatation occurs much faster. The active phase of labour occurs when the cervix is dilated between
5 and 10cm.
Labour is divided into four stages:

First Stage: This stage begins with the onset of strong, painful, and regular uterine contractions, leading to
the dilation of the cervix and the appearance of the show. It should not last for more than 12 hours.

Second Stage: It starts from full dilatation of the cervix to the expulsion of the baby and should not last
for more than 1 hour.

Third Stage: This stage begins after the delivery of the baby and continues until the complete expulsion
of the placenta and membranes, as well as the control of bleeding, which should not last for more than 30
minutes.

Fourth Stage: It encompasses the first hour after delivery, during which both the mother and baby are
allowed to rest and are closely observed. This period is critical, as complications can occur.

MANAGEMENT OF NORMAL LABOUR




SECTION SIX
    First Stage of Labour:
    • Explain the condition to the client.
    • Explain all procedures and seek permission for examination.
    • Ensure and respect the privacy of the woman.
    • Discuss findings with the client and keep her informed about the progress of labour.
    • Encourage active movement alternating with rest between contractions and frequent emptying of the
    bladder.
    • Encourage the woman to bathe or wash herself and her genitals at the onset of labour.
    • Ensure infection prevention.
    • Avoid vaginal examination during contractions.
    • Do not shave the perineal area.
    • Do not administer an enema during labour.
    • Start a Labour Care Guide when labour is in the active phase.
    • Allow the client to choose a more comfortable position.

    Second Stage of Labour:
    • Continue to monitor maternal and fetal vital signs.
    • Swab the vulva with a mild antiseptic lotion.
    • Discourage bearing down until the head is at the perineum (teach the woman to breathe through the
    mouth to prevent pushing before the head has distended to the perineum).
    • Ensure controlled delivery of the head by taking the following precautions:
    • Keep a hand on the head as it advances with contractions.
    • Cover the anus with a perineal pad using your other hand.
    • Observe the perineum for signs of tear.
    • Ask the mother to breathe deeply and steadily with her mouth open and not to push during the delivery
    of the head.
    • Feel gently around the neck for the presence of the cord; if the cord is loosely felt, deliver the baby through
    the loop, but when it is tight, clamp and cut it, then unwind it from the neck.
    • Allow for natural restitution.
    • Deliver the anterior shoulder by applying gentle pressure downward.
    • Lift the baby upward toward the abdomen to deliver the posterior shoulder and the rest of the body.
    • Place the baby on the mother’s abdomen.
    • Note the time of delivery.
    • Tie/Clamp The Cord in 2 Places and Cut the Center After About 2-3 Minutes.
    • Provide immediate newborn care (Refer to Newborn Section).
    • Avoid the following:
    • Rectal examination.
    • Lithotomy position with or without stirrups.
    • Fundal pressure during labour.

Third Stage of Labour (Use Active Management of Third Stage of Labour - AMSTL):
• Palpate the abdomen to rule out the presence of another baby.
• Administer a uterotonic IM oxytocin 20mg on the thigh within one minute after birth or IM or IV Carbetocin

Perform Controlled Cord Traction (CCT) as follows:
• Clamp the cord close to the perineum.
• Use the other hand to apply counter traction on the uterus, just above the pubic bone.
• Use a forward and downward motion to deliver the placenta.
• Maintain steady but gentle pressure.
• Cup the placenta in your hands and deliver it gently once it is visible at the perineum to ensure that the
membranes are intact.
• Examine the placenta and membranes to ensure completeness.
• Clean the mother and check the perineum for laceration or bruising.

Fourth Stage of Labour:
• Massage the uterus every 15 minutes for the next 2 hours to ensure that it is well contracted.
• Continue to check vital signs.
• Ensure breastfeeding is initiated to promote bonding.
• Monitor the amount of bleeding.
• Observe for any danger signs in both mother and newborn.
• Teach the mother about perineal care.

Best Practices:
• Close monitoring during the first 6 hours.
• Monitor BP, Pulse, Vaginal bleeding, and uterine hardness; every 15 minutes for 2 hours, every 30 minutes
for 1 hour, and every hour for the last 3 hours.

6.3 POSTNATAL DISORDERS



    SECTION SIX
The postnatal period is 6-8 weeks after delivery. This is the time that lactation is well established and the
return of the uterus and other reproductive organs to the pre-gravid state. At least 3 postnatal visits for
checkup should be encouraged to determine how the mother and baby are faring. This is also a special
opportunity to screen postnatal mothers for signs of mental health conditions such as; puerperal psychosis,
involvement in substance use/abuse and signs of domestic violence, for appropriate management and referral.
