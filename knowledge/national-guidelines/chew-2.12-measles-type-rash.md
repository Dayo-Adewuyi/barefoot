2.12. MEASLES – TYPE RASH
    Measles is an acute viral infection characterized by the appearance of rashes in small groups consisting of
    macules (flat spots) and papules (raised spots). It typically begins with high fever, cough, nasal congestion,
    redness of the eyes, and inflammation of the mucous membranes of the mouth. Later stages may include
    mouth sores, followed by the appearance of rashes across the entire body after approximately the fourth
    day of illness onset.




SECTION TWO
    In addition to the characteristic rash, children with measles may also experience other serious complications
    such as pneumonia, diarrhea, or malnutrition, either concurrently or shortly after the onset of the disease.
    Measles can be particularly severe in poorly nourished children and may lead to various complications,
    underscoring the importance of timely intervention and appropriate medical care.
