SECTION TWO
2.30. CHANGES IN ALERTNESS AND BEHAVIOUR
Changes in alertness and behavior mean that the client does not act normal. He may be drowsy or
unconscious or give a history of fainting. Big changes such as unconsciousness or delirium are easy to identify.
Small changes such as drowsiness and decreased alertness are not easily noticed. If the clients is not alert or
is drowsy, it may not be noticed easily if this happens, the client may not receive treatment and could come
back later in more serious condition. Therefore, careful observation of client is important.

SECTION TWO
2.31. POISONING
A poisoning is a toxic substance which enters the body through swallowing, breathing, absorption through
the skin or mucous membrane or infection. Poison may be accidental, suicidal or homicidal. An overdose of a
medicinal drug may act as poison e.g. overdose of aspirin. It is common for children under 5 years of age to
accidentally ingest medicine not prescribed for them or over dosage of prescribed, drugs; alcohol (gin),
kerosene, petrol or corrosive substance e.g. acid, lead or bleaches.

2.32. EMERGENCY CONDITIONS
Children 2 months to 5 years can be threatening and should be treated promptly to save the life of the child.
There should be no hesitation in asking for or referring where necessary. Severe acute fever, diarrhea,
malnutrition, child poisoning, conjunctivitis, epilepsy, choking and anaphylaxis are emergency situations and
should be treated as soon as possible to save the life of the child.
