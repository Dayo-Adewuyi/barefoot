6.6 PRE-MENOPAUSE
Pre-menopause is a natural process of transitioning to the menopausal stage when the woman’s period stops
as a result of advancement in age marking the end of reproductive years. It is marked with changes in the
menstrual cycle, along with other physical and emotional symptoms. The symptoms are caused by changes in
the hormonal levels in the body. The pre-menopausal period can last between 2-10 years before menopause
set in.

7.0 GERIATRIC HEALTH

    The World Health Organization (WHO) describes the elderly as persons aged 60 years and above. Thus,
    with an increasing number of elderly persons (65 years and above) in the population, the PHC worker will
    be confronted with a growing number of elderly patients. This group of patients is vulnerable and at risk of
    health, nutritional, and social problems. They have spent most of their lives contributing to nation-building,
    either in the public or private sector when they were younger, so they deserve the best care from the
    country they served. There are differences in the signs and symptoms of diseases presented in the elderly
    compared to younger people. Therefore, diagnosis in the elderly may require a more precise history and
    laboratory tests. Due to changes associated with the ageing process, such as difficulty in hearing, speech
    problems, memory loss, and so on, it may be necessary for a family member to be involved in their care. An
    understanding of the needs of the elderly is essential for PHC workers.


    7.1. MUSCULO - SKELETAL SYSTEM DISORDERS
    The musculoskeletal system is the collective term for the interconnected system of muscles, bones, joints,
    ligaments, tendons, and other connective tissues in the body. It provides structural support, allows movement,
    protects internal organs, and plays a vital role in maintaining postures. The muscles, controlled by the nervous
    system to enable various bodily functions, such as walking, lifting, and other forms of physical activity. This
    system is essential for overall body function and mobility.
    As adults age, they begin to lose minerals and living materials from their bones. The bones become weaker
    and even more brittle. This physiological process makes the elderly suffer from more broken bones.
    Osteoarthritis is a disease that occurs with ageing and in this condition normal joint movement becomes
    painful and difficult.
