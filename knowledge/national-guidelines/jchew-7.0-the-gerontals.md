# JCHEW Standing Orders 7.0: The Gerontals (elderly, 60 years and over)

> Source: *National Standing Orders for Junior Community Health Extension Workers*, 2024 revision, section 7.0, pages 337-337.
> Applies to **JCHEW** workers only, for patients in the **elderly, 60 years and over** group.

<!-- page 337 -->

7.0 GERIATRIC HEALTH




    SECTION SEVEN
The World Health Organization (WHO) defines the elderly as people whose age is 60 years and above.
It’s important to note that there are a significant and growing number of elderly persons in the population.
This means that the PHC worker can expect an increasing number of elderly patients. This group of patients
(clients) are vulnerable and at risk of having chronic diseases such as hypertension, diabetes, osteomyelitis,
cataracts, nyctalopia, hearing impairment, dementia, nutrition and social problems. They spent most of their
lives in nation-building, either in the public or in the private sector, when they were much younger. They
deserve the best of care from the country they served.
There are differences in the signs and symptoms of diseases presented in the elderly and young people.
Therefore, diagnosis in the elderly may require more precise history/laboratory tests. Due to some changes
associated with the ageing process, such as difficulty in hearing, speech problems, memory loss, etc., it would
be necessary for a family member, who plays a crucial role in the care of the elderly, to be involved in their
care. An understanding of the needs of the elderly is essential for PHC workers, as is the recognition of the
invaluable support that family members can provide in this process.

7.1. MUSCULO - SKELETAL SYSTEM DISORDERS

As adults age, they begin to lose minerals and living materials from their bones. The bones become weaker
and even more brittle. This
physiological process makes the elderly suffer from more broken bones. Osteoarthritis is a disease that
occurs with ageing and in this condition normal joint movement becomes painful and difficult.
