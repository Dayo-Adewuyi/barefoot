```text
2.3. FEVER
Fever is elevation of body temperature above 37.5°C. Fever is a sign of infection or loss of body fluid. Rapid
rise of temperature may lead to fits especially in young children of 6 months to 5 years (febrile convulsion).
Some common causes of fever are malaria, tuberculosis, respiratory tract infections, gastrointestinal and
other bacterial and viral infections. All patients with a history of fever should be treated and encouraged to
drink plenty of water, other fluids and fruits. High fever should be treated as an emergency.
```

```text
SECTION TWO
Health Education
Advise mother/caregiver on:

1. Environmental sanitation and prevention of disease transmission (Vector control, drainage)
2. Proper housing condition (ventilation, lighting)
3. Use of long-lasting insecticide net (LLIN)/ material for malaria control
4.Safe disposal of animal and human bodies infected with Lassa virus, SARS virus and Ebola
5. Wearing protective clothing when around person with the disease
6. Personal and food hygiene
7. Encourage fluid intake
8. Nutrition counseling
9. Safe water and disease prevention
10. Importance of immunization in disease prevention and control
11. Importance of taking home-based records on child’s next visit to the clinic or hospital
```

```text
    2.4. CONVULSION
    Convulsion is a jerky movement of the arms and legs and twitching of muscles. It is very common in infants
    and children and can be caused by high fever from any cause, head injury, tumour or sometimes
    infections of the brain or spinal cord. Muscle twitching due to tetanus or lock jaw should not be
    mistaken for convulsion. Tetanus often begins with tightness of the mouth jaw and muscle twitching.
```
